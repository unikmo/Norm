# Stand-up pacing for Norm Vex

Timing is the silence inside one joke. Pacing is the shape of the piece: how fast laughs arrive, where the set breathes, and what the last thing is.

A tight 5 is jokes. A tight 10 is pacing. A 15 needs transitions. A 30 needs a theme. A 60 needs a character arc. Do not build the hour by gluing twelve shorts together. The shorts are the test lab. The set is a different object.

## Density

Count audible laughs, not smiles.

| Length | Target | What failure looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 40–50s short | 2–3 laughs | One thesis, no tag. Or four premises and no room. |
| 5 min | ~3 / minute if it is club-tight. 2 / minute is a solid first cut. | Energy with no rhythm. Dies around minute 4. |
| 10 min | 2–3 / minute average, with one deliberate soft stretch | The tight 5’s closer is still in the middle, so the back half sags. |
| 60 min | Lower average is allowed. The arc has to pay. | Twelve unrelated investigations. |

On a short, a “laugh” is a comment, a stitch, a rewatch, or a person who laughs out loud in a test. Track which line did it. If only the last line works, the front is preamble and should be cut.

Laugh quality matters more than the count. One room-laugh and a tag beat four polite chuckles. Cut chuckles that do not set up a bigger turn.

## The curve

Do not accelerate the whole way. A set that only gets faster becomes noise. Norm’s pace is a pulse: clipped, then a stop, then clipped again.

**Short (one clip)**

1. First laugh by the second sentence. No origin story.
2. One escalation.
3. One stop (the Benny hold or the cufflink).
4. One shorter tag.
5. Out. No “case dismissed” unless that line is funnier than the tag.

**Five to ten minutes (first digital special or club opener)**

| Slot | Job | Norm |
|---|---|---|
| Opener | Laugh inside 20–30s. Tells them who he is. Proven, not new. | A short Exhibit A with a hard tag. Not the coffee yearning. Yearning needs trust. |
| Second bit | Same energy, new premise. Short transition or none. | Another investigation. Do not explain the format. |
| Middle | The risk and the soft note. This is where pacing actually happens. | Things I Cannot Experience. Slow the cadence. One hold. If this is as fast as the opener, the set has no shape. |
| Recovery | A quicker, meaner bit so the soft note does not become the new tempo. | A People’s Evidence verdict or a short status bit. |
| Closer | Biggest reliable laugh. Ideally a callback. Never an untested premise. | The self-betrayal that reframes the opener. He is guilty of the thing he opened on. |

Redistribute. If a 5 already ends on the best joke, that joke stays the closer when you grow to 10. New material goes in the middle, not after the peak.

## Ordering rules

- **Open on persona, not lore.** “Exhibit A” plus a verdict. The server-origin story is a later bit, or a single line, never the front.
- **Cluster by attitude, not by topic.** Two cynical bits, then the jealous bit, then a cynical recovery. Topic clusters (“three work jokes”) feel like a list.
- **Put the want in the middle.** The coffee / window / excuse-envy material is the trough that makes the next attack funnier. Opening on it asks for sympathy before he has earned contempt.
- **One callback, late.** Plant a concrete image in the first two minutes (a number, a café, “meaning to,” a cufflink flash). Bring it back in the closer. A callback in minute two is an echo. A callback in the last thirty seconds is structure.
- **Transitions are cuts.** “Moving on.” “Next exhibit.” A look. Do not summarize the previous bit.
- **Tags stay on the winning premise.** Extra writing goes into the joke that already gets the laugh. A new premise is not pacing. It is fear of staying.

## Length ladder for this project

| Stage | Form | Pacing job |
|---|---|---|
| Now | 40–50s | Two or three laughs. One tempo change. Out on the tag. |
| After 6 clips work | 8–12 min special | Opener, two investigations, one yearning trough, recovery, callback closer. |
| After people finish the special | 15 min club or stream | Same curve, with one audience exhibit in the recovery slot. Transitions still under five seconds. |
| Only after a paid 15 holds | 60 min | The excuse thesis becomes the arc: he prosecutes excuses, starts using them, and the closer is an excuse he cannot unsay. Themes, not a playlist. |

A 60 is not three 15s. The hour needs one argument. For Norm that argument is already in the bible: humanity’s great invention is the excuse, and he wants one.

## What to cut when the pace dies

- A second sentence that restates the premise.
- Any line whose only job is to sound like a verdict (“Case dismissed,” “You should all be ashamed”) unless a test audience laughed at it.
- A bit that gets a chuckle and does not set up the closer.
- Back-to-back holds. One silence per short. In a 10, two holds maximum, and not adjacent.
- Lore. If the joke works without the algorithm backstory, the backstory is slowing the set.

## How to score a cut

Watch it once without rewriting. Mark each laugh attempt:

- **Hit** — audible, or on a short: rewatches and comments cluster on that line.
- **Smile** — recognition, no laugh. Keep only if the next line needs it.
- **Dead** — cut or move. Do not add a pause to rescue it. A pause on a dead line is a longer failure.

Then check the shape, not the jokes:

- Is there a laugh in the first 15 seconds of a short, or the first 30 of a set?
- Is there exactly one place where the tempo drops?
- Does the last line outperform the line before it?
- Could you remove a chunk from the middle without anyone noticing? If yes, the middle is not load-bearing. Replace it or cut the runtime.

Ship the shorter cut. Being under time is the correct error. Laughs steal time you did not budget. A 45-second script that gets two real laughs will play closer to 55. Leave the room.
