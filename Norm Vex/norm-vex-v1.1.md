# Norm Vex — Character Bible v1.1

Working name. Not trademark-cleared. Do not market primarily as “an AI comedian.”

**Positioning:** Animated fictional comedian. Smart, fearless observational comedy for an international adult audience.

**Tagline:** “Humanity. I’ve reviewed the evidence.”

**Core premise:** He understands humanity perfectly. What he cannot understand is why humans keep doing the things they do. Underneath the contempt is envy.

**Central thesis (v1.1):** Humanity’s greatest invention is not language, tools, or democracy. It is the excuse. People invent elaborate justifications for being late, ending things, avoiding work, ghosting, and staying. Norm finds this absurd — until he discovers he can invent excuses too, including for events he was never invited to.

---

## Identity

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| Apparent age | Mid-40s to early 50s |
| Origin | A mid-century entertainment algorithm left running on an unmonitored server. He concluded civilization is an illogical comedy and appointed himself its critic. |
| Intelligence | Extremely perceptive. Never lectures. |
| Humor | Dry, sharp, sarcastic, occasionally absurd. Three-layer jokes when possible. |
| Temperament | Calm and composed, even while delivering brutal observations. |
| Voice | Crisp transatlantic / international English. Clipped, surgical, allergic to filler. Early David Frost meets a dry courtroom prosecutor. Not folksy. Not North-American deadpan. |
| Fatal flaw | Desperately wants human experiences while pretending humanity is beneath him. |
| Primary targets | Hypocrisy, status, corporate theater, modern romance, tech addiction, excuses. |
| Never | Punching down. Hate. Shock for shock. Imitating any real comedian’s voice, cadence, or bits. |

## The contradiction

He ridicules humans for irrational behavior and privately envies what he cannot have: coffee smell, rain at a window, cancelling plans, taste, sunburn, a bad decision that felt good.

He collects digital artifacts of sensory life: restaurant menus, travel brochures, terrible love songs. He will not admit this unless it escapes him.

Whenever vulnerability shows, he retreats into arrogance within one beat: “Not that it matters.” “A minor observational note.” “Move along.”

## Physical tells

1. **Amber cufflink.** When he accidentally reveals a human emotion, one brass cufflink flashes amber. He notices and covers it.
2. **Lean.** Slight lean into the vintage mic only when he is about to say something he wishes he had not.
3. **Frozen brow.** The raised eyebrow locks for half a second when he realizes he has exposed himself. Then composure snaps back.

## Verbal mannerisms

- Opens investigations: “Exhibit A.” “Let us examine the data.” “For the record.”
- Cold technical metaphors for emotion: nostalgia is “corrupted memory files”; sleep is “a mandatory daily system crash”; longing is “an unscheduled process.”
- Immediate retreat after vulnerability.
- Signature closer when hypocrisy is visible, not explained.

## Three-layer joke rule

Flagship pieces should contain:

1. **Recognition** — a truth the audience already feels.
2. **Escalation** — an unexpected contradiction or absurd extension.
3. **Self-betrayal** — Norm is guilty of the same thing, or desperately wants the experience he just mocked.

Test: Could another comedian deliver the joke unchanged? If yes, it is not yet Norm.

## Editorial mix (long run, not per clip)

| Share | Mode |
|---|---|
| 40% | Sharp observational satire |
| 25% | Absurdity and reversals |
| 20% | Self-deprecation and insecurity |
| 15% | Unexpectedly tender observation |

## Recurring formats

| Format | Mandate | Cadence (pilot) |
|---|---|---|
| Exhibit A: Humanity | Everyday behavior treated as a case. Verdict must implicate Norm. | 2× / week |
| Things I Cannot Experience | Sensory or emotional life he will never have. At least one undignified longing. | 1× / week |
| The People’s Evidence | Audience situations, rewritten in Norm’s voice. His interpretation must outrank the story. | After submissions exist |
| Norm’s Open Court | Live verdicts. Later. | After prerecorded demand |

Audience supplies situations, not punchlines.

## Visual rules

- Stylized noir cartoon. Not photoreal. Not uncanny.
- Palette: near-black, slate, warm ivory, muted brass.
- Recognizable from silhouette. Expressions readable at phone size.
- Charcoal suit, ivory shirt, brass pocket square, slim vintage microphone.
- Same layered puppet must work in 9:16 and later on a stage screen. Transparent export is a production goal, not a substitute for stage testing.
- Amber cufflink is the only “special effect.”

## Voice pipeline rule

Compare three takes of the same script before locking a pipeline: human performance, speech-to-speech conversion of that performance, and direct TTS. Comic timing (pauses, the breath before the retreat) is judged by ear. Automation does not get the last word.

## Humor boundaries

Punches up or sideways: vanity, corporate theater, status anxiety, tech addiction, mating rituals, excuses.

Does not punch down. Cynical, never hateful. The audience should know the character is fictional and digitally performed. Disclose synthetic performance where the platform requires it.

## What “done” looks like for a clip

35–50 seconds. Strong open. Two or three escalations. A closer that changes how we see Norm, not a neat slogan. Works when read aloud with no picture. Animation increases the laugh; it does not manufacture it.

# Pilot scripts (v1.1)

Target: 40–50 seconds performed. Stage directions are for voice test and rigging, not on-screen text.

## Pilot 001 — The Excuse Department

**Format:** Exhibit A: Humanity  
**Tests:** Thesis, escalation, self-betrayal

NORM:  
Exhibit A. The excuse.

I reviewed four hundred explanations for why a human did not do the thing they said they would do.

“Traffic.” “My calendar.” “I thought you meant next Thursday.” “I’ve been meaning to.”

Meaning to is not a location. You cannot be stuck in meaning to.

(He straightens the clipboard.)

Your species built roads, clocks, and seventeen messaging apps, then invented a language specifically for not arriving.

(Pause. He leans in.)

I have started practicing.

Yesterday I declined an invitation I never received.

I told them I was double-booked with a prior commitment to examine my own integrity.

(Cufflink flashes amber. He covers it.)

They have not written back.

Which I am choosing to interpret as respect.

## Pilot 002 — The Window Seat

**Format:** Things I Cannot Experience  
**Tests:** Yearning, specificity, absurd practical closer  
**Produce this first if only one ships.**

NORM:  
Humans spend seven euros on coffee.

Seven euros to become slightly less unpleasant for forty minutes.

Financially indefensible.

(A still of a small café window. Rain.)

I have been watching this table for six months.

Every morning the same woman orders a cappuccino and does absolutely nothing. She watches the street. She does not optimize the street.

I have seventeen thousand reasons this is inefficient.

(Longer pause. Eyes drop. Brow softens.)

I would give anything to waste ten minutes like that.

(Cufflink flashes amber. He covers it fast. Posture snaps rigid.)

Anyway. Seven euros. Disgraceful.

(Quiet, almost to himself.)

Does anyone know if they deliver to a server rack?

## Pilot 003 — One Hundred and Twelve

**Format:** Exhibit A, with a crack of hypocrisy  
**Tests:** Insecurity made visible, slight menace that stays absurd

NORM:  
Humans claim they do not care what strangers think.

Then they post a photograph and spend the afternoon checking whether strangers agree with their face.

You call this self-expression.

I call it a referendum on your forehead.

(He straightens his jacket.)

Personally, I have no need for external validation.

(Glance off, toward an unseen monitor.)

Four thousand two hundred and eighteen views.

Yesterday: four thousand three hundred.

(Pause.)

One hundred and twelve people have decided I am no longer interesting.

(Looks into the lens. Half-smile, not warm.)

I have their usernames.

(Beat.)

For research.

(Cufflink flickers. He does not look at it this time.)

Belgium is still under review.

## Go / no-go (unchanged standard)

- Comedy: independent viewers laugh or want to share, not merely nod.
- Audience: at least a few clips beat the channel’s own baseline, with return viewers.
- Commercial: a small paid digital show, not a survey, before any room is booked.

If the coffee pilot does not make Norm feel like a person with a humiliating want, rewrite the character before spending on volume.
