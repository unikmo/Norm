# Video editing for Norm Vex

The edit is the last timing pass. A voice model will not land the joke. The cut will.

Two clocks fight each other. Short-form advice says trim every inhale, because dead air kills watch-through. Stand-up says the pause after the punch is the joke. Both are right, on different frames. Cut air that is only a breath. Keep air that was marked in the timing script. If you tighten the Benny hold because a retention graph dipped, you cut the laugh to save the viewer who was never going to laugh.

## The cut list

| Cut | Use | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| Hard cut | Default. New line, new exhibit, end card. | Dissolves, whip pans, glitch, zoom-through. Those read as a creator, not a comic. |
| Jump cut | Remove a fluff inhale or a repeated word inside a line. | Jump the face during a hold. The expression has to stay continuous or the slip looks like a glitch. |
| Hold | After a punch, on the face or the cufflink. | Hold on a dead line to “give it a chance.” |
| Cut on the consonant | Start the next line on a hard sound. | Cut on a vowel tail. It sounds mushy and steps on the last word. |
| J-cut, rare | The next line’s first word starts under the last frames of the hold, only if the hold has already peaked. | Use it on the coffee hold or a callback click. Those need silence. |
| Smash to black | The out-point, on the fade of the last hold. | A button, a logo sting, or “case dismissed” after the tag. |

End after the laugh has peaked and started to fall, not on the last phoneme. On a 45-second clip that is about one second past the word for a normal punch, and closer to two for a callback, because that laugh arrives late. Then out. Do not show him resetting his face for the next take.

## What the picture does while he is quiet

A silent face with no change is a frozen file. A marked pause needs one of these, and only one:

- The cufflink flashes and he covers it. The cover is the cut-in. Hold the covered wrist, not a zoom.
- Eyes drop, then snap back. Stay on the same framing so the snap reads.
- A glance off-camera to the monitor. Do not show the monitor numbers unless the joke is the number. If you show the number, put it up as he looks, and take it away before he speaks again.
- Nothing moves, on purpose, for the Benny hold. The stillness is the event. Do not add a push-in. A push-in sells the moment and makes it smaller.

One picture-punch per clip. Two and the effects become the act.

## Captions

Caption the first line, short, so a muted scroll knows the premise. Do not caption the punch. Text on the laugh word pulls the eye off the face, which is the only reaction shot you have. Burned-in captions on every line turn him into a quote account.

If a test watch with sound off does not read as a joke, the face and the hold failed. Do not fix that with a caption on the tag. Fix the hold.

No emoji, no arrows, no “wait for it.”

## Sound

- Room tone under the whole clip, very low. Pure digital silence makes the pause feel like a dropout.
- No laugh track. No stinger on the punch. A sound effect on the cufflink is allowed only if it is quieter than the room and happens once.
- Music bed: none in the pilot. A bed tells the viewer when to feel something. That is the caption problem in audio.
- Level the voice so the quiet tag (“server rack,” “for research”) is still intelligible. Do not ride the volume up into the yearning line. The drop in energy is the performance. Compressing it flat is an accidental rewrite.
- Leave the breaths you marked. Delete the breaths you did not.

## Assembly order

1. Lock the voice edit first, with the pauses from the timing script already in the audio. Do not animate until the track makes you wait.
2. Lay the puppet to that track. Mouth and brows follow the words. The face does not anticipate the punch.
3. Place the one picture-punch on the marked hold.
4. Trim the head so the first word is inside the first second. No logo, no “Exhibit A” title card before he speaks. He can say it.
5. Set the out-point on the fall of the last hold.
6. Watch once with sound, once without. Sound pass: did you talk over the late laugh? Silent pass: does the face change exactly once, at the hold?
7. Export 9:16 from the same timeline as a 16:9 master. Do not re-time the wide version. Reframe only.

## Jump cuts and this character

He is one figure on a stick. A jump cut in the middle of a clinical line is fine and even useful. It makes the investigation feel edited, like evidence. A jump cut across the slip breaks the cufflink, because the viewer has to see the flash and the cover in one uninterrupted move. Protect that span. Cut around it, not through it.

## What not to add because other comedy clips do

Split screens, comment screenshots, stock “mind blown,” progress bars, subscribe buttons over the tag, a replay of the punch in slow motion. Props and exhibits can come later, as objects he holds up. They are not edit effects. If the joke needs a screenshot to be clear, the joke is not ready. The exception already in the scripts is a single café still or a single number, on screen only while he is looking at it.

## The coffee clip, as an edit

Fast picture through the seven-euro case. No cuts inside “I would give anything to waste ten minutes like that,” except the eyes. Hold. Cufflink. Cover. Hard return to the fast framing on “Anyway.” The server-rack line is quieter, same frame, no caption. Hold. Black on the fall. Runtime will grow past the script. Leave it.
